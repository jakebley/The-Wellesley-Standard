# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

People considering a move to Wellesley, MA, and current residents who want to feel plugged into the town — searching things like "what's going on in Wellesley this weekend" or "what is good about Wellesley Massachusetts." Also local advertisers (real estate, food & beverage) and prospective "House of the Week" / sponsored-feature subjects. Casual, browsing mindset — not task-driven; they're here to feel the town's texture, not complete a transaction.

## Product Purpose

1881 is a magazine-style media and events site for Wellesley, Massachusetts — named for the town's founding year. It is explicitly not a news outlet or blog. It covers the town's homes, streets, events, and everyday civic life with editorial polish. Success = ranking #1 for the target local searches above, and reading as a clear step up in production quality from the town's existing coverage.

## Brand Personality

Polished, editorial, understated-affluent. Should read like a small, confident city magazine — not a real estate blog, not a civic newsletter. Trustworthy without being stuffy; warm without being cutesy.

## Anti-references

**The Swellesley Report** (the town's existing local blog) — explicitly named as the thing 1881 is a polish step up from: messy, blog-like, ad-hoc layout. 1881 should never read as a blog feed. Also avoid: real-estate-listing aesthetics (price tags, sq ft, agent contact info — House of the Week is editorial, not a listing) and generic small-town-news templates.

## Design Principles

- Editorial, not blog — every page reads as magazine layout, not a reverse-chronological feed
- Show the town's texture, don't just list facts — imagery and specific detail over generic copy
- Sponsor content stays visually distinct from editorial (never blur the two)
- Recurring content pillars (House of the Week, Streets of Wellesley) are the SEO and identity engine — their consistency matters more than any single page's novelty
- Restraint over decoration — the black/paper/red identity carries the brand; don't reach for extra ornament to compensate

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA baseline. No additional accessibility needs specified by the user; `prefers-reduced-motion` support is already built into the base styles and should be preserved as motion work is added.
